# Challenge 2: Help when it's needed

## Connecting people affected by substance use with support, out of hours

Based on [CivTech Challenge 12.1](https://www.civtech.scot/civtech-12-challenges), sponsored by the Digital Health & Care Innovation Centre (DHI), Simon Community Scotland and With You.

### The problem

Scotland has the highest rates of drug and alcohol deaths in the UK and Europe. Most treatment and support services for people with substance use issues are open Monday to Friday, 9 to 5, and are mostly in cities and larger towns. But people are often at their most vulnerable and isolated at night. Support is spread across lots of organisations, is hard to find and varies from place to place. When help isn't there, or isn't trusted, people and their families turn to emergency services or get no support at all, and situations can escalate into a crisis.

### Who's affected

- **People living with substance use issues**
- **Their families and friends**, the "circle of support" around them
- **Support workers, charities and community organisations**, such as Simon Community Scotland and With You
- **Local Alcohol and Drug Partnerships**, the NHS and the Scottish Ambulance Service

### What gets in the way

- Most help closes in the evening and at weekends, often exactly when it's needed.
- Services are concentrated in cities. Living rurally, or having no transport, little money or poor health, makes them harder to reach.
- Support is spread across many organisations and varies by area, so it's hard to know what exists, especially somewhere else.
- Stigma, and low trust in statutory services, stop people reaching out.
- Phones get in the way too: limited data, battery or signal, no suitable device, or low confidence with technology.
- Families and friends often don't know how to help or where to turn.

### What people need

- To reach **trusted, non-judgemental support** when they need it, wherever they are.
- To **feel safer and less alone**, especially at night.
- **Families and friends** to be part of the support, and to be supported themselves.
- **Services** to pool what they have so there's cover out of hours, and to learn where the gaps are.

### Things to keep in mind

- **Not another service directory or call centre.** Either might be part of an answer, but the sponsors don't want that to be the answer.
- **Supportive, not an emergency.** It shouldn't feel like a crisis service, and it isn't a replacement for 999.
- **Trust, stigma and privacy.** Substance use is very sensitive (special category) information.
- **Safety and risk.**
- **People with limited phone data, battery or signal.**
- **Person-first language:** "a person who uses drugs", not "an addict".

### Useful starting points

- Scotland's alcohol and drugs strategic plan 2026 to 2035, *Preventing Harm, Promoting Recovery*, puts the person's "circle of support" at the centre.
- DHI runs the Digital Lifelines Scotland programme.
