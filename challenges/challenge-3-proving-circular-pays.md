# Challenge 3: Proving circular pays

## Making the business case for the circular economy

Based on [CivTech Challenge 12.3](https://www.civtech.scot/civtech-12-challenges), sponsored by Zero Waste Scotland.

### The problem

A circular economy keeps products and materials in use for longer, through things like reuse, repair, leasing, and selling access to something rather than ownership of it. Scotland wants more businesses to work this way. But many businesses, especially small ones, find it hard to show that circular practices make commercial sense. Traditional financial tools are built for "make, use, throw away" businesses. They tend to miss benefits like a more reliable supply chain, secure access to materials, customers who stay loyal for longer, and less exposure to rising material and energy costs. Those benefits often take time to appear, or are spread across several businesses, so circular ideas can look weaker than they are and are harder to get investment for.

### Who's affected

- **Small business owners and entrepreneurs**
- **Investors, funders and banks**
- **Business support advisers**, such as Business Gateway and the enterprise agencies
- **Zero Waste Scotland**, its business support, policy and research teams
- **Scottish Government policymakers**

### What gets in the way

- Investors and banks judge circular ideas with tools built for traditional businesses.
- The benefits are hard to put numbers on, slow to show up, or shared across several businesses.
- Small businesses have little time or money for detailed analysis.
- Zero Waste Scotland's one-to-one business support worked well, but takes too many people to reach everyone.
- Business figures are confidential, so owners are wary of sharing them.

### What people need

- **Business owners:** a quick way to make a case for a circular idea that an investor will believe.
- **Investors:** numbers, methods and assumptions they can trust and compare.
- **Zero Waste Scotland:** to learn from what businesses share, anonymised and combined, to improve its support and policies.

### Things to keep in mind

- **Business owners have little time.**
- **Numbers investors will trust**, with the assumptions shown.
- **Keep business information confidential.**
- **A static spreadsheet or PDF won't be enough.**
- **It's about commercial value.** A purely environmental or social impact tool is out of scope.

### Useful starting points

- Scotland's Circular Economy Strategy, and the Circular Economy (Scotland) Act 2024.
- Zero Waste Scotland's Business Information Hub, where a tool like this might live.
